# Display Technology & Touch Input — Study Notes

## 1. Touchscreen & Digitizer

### Touchscreen
- Detects **finger input**
- Commonly used for navigation and gestures

#### Capacitive Touchscreens
- Most common today (phones, tablets)
- Detect touch via **electrical conductivity of the human body**
- Highly responsive
- Supports **multi-touch gestures** (pinch, zoom, swipe)
- Does **not** work well with gloves unless designed for it

#### Resistive Touchscreens
- Detect input via **pressure between two conductive layers**
- Works with gloves, stylus, pens, or any pointed object
- Less sensitive
- Does **not** support multi-touch
- Often used in industrial or medical environments

---

### Digitizer
- Detects **stylus input**
- Enables precise drawing or handwriting
- Common in creative tablets and 2-in-1 laptops
- Touchscreen ≠ digitizer (they are separate components)

---

## 2. Display Attributes

### Pixel Density (PPI)
- Pixels Per Inch (PPI)
- Determines **sharpness and clarity**
- Higher PPI → sharper text and images
- Lower PPI → blocky or pixelated visuals

**Key Concept:**
- Same resolution on a **smaller screen = higher PPI**
- Example:
  - 1080p looks sharp on a 5-inch screen
  - 1080p looks blurry on a 32-inch display

**Best for:**
- Reading
- Photo editing
- Detailed graphics work

---

### Refresh Rate (Hz)
- Measures how many times per second the screen updates
- Higher Hz = smoother motion

#### Common Refresh Rates
- 60 Hz → Standard (office, movies)
- 120 Hz → Smooth scrolling, premium devices
- 144–240 Hz → Gaming / high performance

**Use Case Breakdown**
- Office / Productivity → 60 Hz
- Movies / Streaming → 60 Hz
- Casual Gaming → 75–120 Hz
- Competitive Gaming → 144–240 Hz

---

### Screen Resolution
- Total number of pixels (width × height)

#### Common Standards
- HD (720p) → 1280 × 720
- Full HD (1080p) → 1920 × 1080
- QHD (1440p) → 2560 × 1440
- 4K (2160p) → 3840 × 2160
- 8K → 7680 × 4320

**Key Rule:**
- Resolution ≠ screen size
- Same resolution on different screen sizes = different sharpness

---

### Color Gamut
- Range of colors a display can produce

#### Common Standards
- sRGB → Standard for web & general use
- Adobe RGB → Wider range, ideal for photography & design
- DCI-P3 → Used in HDR and modern smartphones

**Wider gamut =**
- Richer colors
- Better color accuracy
- Most noticeable in photo/video editing

---

## 3. Display Panel Types

### LCD Panel Types
- **TN (Twisted Nematic)**
  - Fast response times
  - Lower cost
  - Poor color accuracy & viewing angles
  - Common in competitive gaming

- **IPS (In-Plane Switching)**
  - Excellent color accuracy
  - Wide viewing angles
  - Slightly slower response times
  - Ideal for productivity and creative work

- **VA (Vertical Alignment)**
  - Best contrast among LCDs
  - Deeper blacks than IPS
  - Slower response than TN

---

### OLED
- Pixels emit their **own light**
- True blacks (pixels turn off)
- Infinite contrast
- Vibrant colors
- Risk of **burn-in**
- Common in premium phones and tablets

---

### Mini-LED
- Advanced LCD backlighting technology
- Brighter than standard LCD
- Better contrast than traditional LCD
- No burn-in risk like OLED

---

## 4. Inverter (Legacy Knowledge)
- Converts **DC to AC**
- Required for **CCFL backlit displays**
- Modern LED-backlit displays **do not use inverters**

---

## 5. Real-World Display Examples

### Everyday Laptop
- 13" IPS LCD
- 1920×1080
- ~300 PPI
- 60 Hz
- sRGB
- Balanced for productivity and battery life

---

### Gaming Monitor
- 27" TN LCD
- 1920×1080
- ~82 PPI
- 240 Hz
- Narrow color gamut
- Prioritizes speed over image quality

---

### Creative Tablet
- 12.9" OLED
- 2732×2048
- ~264 PPI
- 120 Hz
- DCI-P3
- Touchscreen + active digitizer
- Ideal for artists and designers

---

## 6. Exam & Practical Takeaways

- Office & Movies → **60 Hz**
- Gaming → **Higher refresh rate matters**
- Pixel density depends on **screen size**
- Touchscreen ≠ digitizer
- OLED = best image quality, but burn-in risk
- TN = speed, IPS = accuracy, VA = contrast
- Modern displays do **not** use inverters

---

## Mental Shortcut
- **Refresh Rate = Smoothness**
- **Resolution = Detail**
- **PPI = Sharpness**
- **Color Gamut = Color Accuracy**
